Fraud Alert Database

Knowledge is your best defense. Learn to recognize common scams before they reach you.

Important Safety Reminder

Banks, police, and government agencies will never ask for your passwords, verification codes, or money transfers over the phone. If someone does, hang up immediately.

! Impersonating Officials

How It Works

Scammers call pretending to be police officers, bank staff, or government officials. They claim your bank account is involved in a crime or that you owe money, and demand immediate payment.

Warning Signs

  • Caller claims to be from the police, court, or tax bureau
  • They ask you to transfer money to a "safe account"
  • They threaten arrest or legal action if you do not comply
  • They tell you not to tell your family

What to Do

  • Hang up immediately — real officials never demand money over the phone
  • Call the official number of the agency they claim to represent
  • Tell your family about the call
! Fake Investment Schemes

How It Works

Scammers invite you to join WeChat or QQ groups promising high returns on investments in gold, stocks, cryptocurrency, or health products. They show fake profits to convince you to invest more, then disappear with your money.

Warning Signs

  • "Guaranteed" high returns with "no risk"
  • Pressure to invest quickly before "the opportunity is gone"
  • Group chats where everyone seems to be making money
  • Requests to download unknown apps to make investments

What to Do

  • Only invest through licensed banks and financial institutions
  • Consult your children before making any investment
  • Remember: if it sounds too good to be true, it is
! Fake Tech Support Calls

How It Works

Someone calls claiming your computer or phone has a virus. They ask you to install software that gives them remote control of your device, then steal your banking information.

Warning Signs

  • Unsolicited calls about computer or phone problems
  • Requests to install remote access software
  • Claims that your device is "sending out viruses"
  • Asking for payment to "fix" the problem

What to Do

  • Never install software at a stranger's request
  • Hang up — real tech companies do not make unsolicited calls
  • If worried, ask your family to check your device
! Lottery & Prize Scams

How It Works

You receive a message or call saying you have won a prize, lottery, or sweepstakes. To claim it, you need to pay a "processing fee" or "tax" first. The prize never arrives.

Warning Signs

  • You "won" a contest you never entered
  • You must pay money to receive your prize
  • Urgent deadlines to claim your winnings
  • Requests for bank details to "deposit" the prize

What to Do

  • Real prizes never require you to pay first
  • Delete the message and block the sender
  • Never share your bank information with strangers
! Fake Links & Phishing Messages

How It Works

You receive a text message or WeChat message with a link that looks like it is from your bank, a delivery company, or a family member. Clicking it takes you to a fake website that steals your login and password.

Warning Signs

  • Messages with links you were not expecting
  • Links that look slightly wrong (extra letters, misspellings)
  • Urgent messages like "Your account will be closed"
  • Messages from "family" asking for money through new numbers

What to Do

  • Never click links in unexpected messages
  • If a "family member" asks for money, call them first to verify
  • Type the website address yourself instead of clicking links

Think You Have Been Targeted?

If you suspect a scam, tell your family immediately. They can help you verify and report it.
